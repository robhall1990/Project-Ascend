# Training Program v2 — General Physical Preparedness
**Aug 2026 – end of Dec 2026 (~20 weeks) | Intermediate lifter | Full commercial gym**

## What changes, and why

The original program optimised for upper body size and strength. Preparedness optimises for something different: **carrying loads, working for hours, and not getting injured.** The overlap is large but not total, and three things invert.

**1. Your endurance base is the asset, not the liability.** Four years of aerobic development is the single most transferable physical quality for any sustained-hardship scenario — moving on foot, working all day, recovering between efforts. You've been treating it as the boring background to the "real" goal. It's the other way round.

**2. Being big is a cost, not just a benefit.** Mass has to be fed and carried. A 95 kg lifter needs meaningfully more daily calories than an 80 kg one and has worse thermoregulation and endurance. In a food-secure environment that's fine; in the scenario you're describing it's a liability. The target is lean, dense, and durable — not maximum size.

**3. Injury resistance outranks everything.** No physio, no imaging, no elective surgery. A blown-out shoulder or a disc injury is the failure mode that actually matters. This means submaximal repeatable work over grinding one-rep maxes, and it means the mobility work is not optional filler.

## What's added

- **Loaded carries** — the highest-transfer gym exercise there is. Grip, trunk, posture, legs, all under load, all while moving.
- **Rucking** — weighted walking. Directly builds the capacity you're describing, uses your existing aerobic base, and costs very little recovery. One session a week, replacing an easy run.
- **Odd-object work** — sandbags don't have handles and neither does anything in the real world. Awkward loads recruit stabilisers barbells never touch.
- **Strength-to-weight** — pull-ups, dips, push-ups. Moving your own bodyweight over and around obstacles.
- **Grip** — trained directly rather than assumed.

## What stays

The 4-day structure, double progression, deloads every 5–6 weeks, and a decent amount of the physique work. Muscle is still the goal — it's armour, it's metabolic reserve, and wanting to look good is a perfectly fine reason to train. This is a shift in emphasis, not a different sport.

## The Week

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Upper Push + Carry |
| 2 | Lower / Load-Bearing |
| 3 | Upper Pull + Grip |
| 4 | Work Capacity / Odd Object |
| +  | 1 ruck (weekly, replaces an easy run) |

### Day 1 — Upper Push + Carry
- Standing Overhead Press — 4x5 *(promoted to the main lift — overhead strength transfers to lifting and placing loads far better than bench)*
- Barbell Bench Press or Weighted Dips — 3x6-8
- Incline DB Press — 3x8-10
- **Farmer's Carry — 4 x 40m**, heavy
- Lateral Raise — 3x12-15

### Day 2 — Lower / Load-Bearing
*Upgraded from maintenance. Carrying anything heavy is legs and back.*
- Back Squat or Front Squat — 4x5
- Trap Bar Deadlift — 3x5
- Loaded Walking Lunge — 3x20m
- Front-Rack or Zercher Carry — 3 x 30m
- Hanging Leg Raise — 3x12-15

### Day 3 — Upper Pull + Grip
- Weighted Pull-up — 4x4-6 *(strength-to-weight: the metric that matters for climbing and hauling)*
- Barbell Row or Chest-Supported Row — 3x8-10
- Single-Arm DB Row — 3x10
- Suitcase Carry — 3 x 40m per side *(brutal for the obliques, directly mimics one-handed carrying)*
- Dead Hang — 3 x max time
- Face Pull — 3x15
- Barbell Curl — 3x8-10

### Day 4 — Work Capacity / Odd Object
*Circuit format, 4-5 rounds, moderate rest. This is the session that builds the ability to work hard for a long time.*
- Sandbag Clean to Shoulder — 5x5
- Sled Push or Drag — 4 x 30m
- Kettlebell Swing — 4x15
- Push-ups — 4 x submax
- Sandbag Carry — 4 x 40m
- Finish: EZ-Bar Curl + Tricep Extension, 3x12 each

### Weekly Ruck
Start at 10 kg for 5 km. Add either 2.5 kg or 1 km every fortnight, not both. Cap around 20 kg — beyond that the injury risk climbs faster than the benefit. Boots, not running shoes. This one session probably has more real-world carryover than any other on the list.

---

## Progression

Same double progression on the main lifts. On carries, progress **distance first, then load**. On the ruck, progress as above.

Keep main lifts at 4-6 reps rather than 1-3. You want repeatable strength with margin, not a maximal single that flirts with injury for a number nobody will ever ask you about.

## Deloads

Every 5-6 weeks, unchanged, and now more important — you've added rucking and carries on top of an existing endurance load. Cumulative fatigue is the realistic threat to this plan, far more than insufficient effort.

## Phases

| Phase | Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundation | 1-4 | Technique, baseline loads, start rucking light |
| 2. Accumulation | 5-9 | Build volume on carries and hypertrophy work |
| 3. Strength | 10-14 | Heavier main lifts, ruck load increases |
| 4. Capacity Peak | 15-18 | Highest work-capacity emphasis on Day 4 |
| Taper | 19-20 | Deload, reassess |

## Two things worth saying plainly

**Strength isn't the skill you're describing.** If physically handling another person is genuinely part of your model, that's a *skill* domain, not a strength domain — and the honest answer is that a year of judo or BJJ at a proper club would do more for it than any amount of pressing. It also happens to be excellent conditioning, a social hobby, and something you can start next week. Worth considering on its own merits.

**Fitness is a small share of the actual variable.** In every real disruption that's been studied — natural disasters, infrastructure failures, wartime conditions — outcomes are driven overwhelmingly by community ties, supplies, location, and medical knowledge. Physical capacity helps, but it's well down the list. If preparedness is genuinely the driver, first aid training and knowing your neighbours are cheaper and higher-yield than anything in this document. None of which is a reason not to train — just don't let the training carry more weight in the plan than it can bear.
